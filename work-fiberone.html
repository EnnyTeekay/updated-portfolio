Mobile + Web Redesign · Case Study

FiberOne Broadband

A complete redesign of a Nigerian broadband self service app and website.

Role
Product Designer & Product Manager
Timeline
3 weeks
Type
Case Study
Overview

The context

FiberOne is a high-speed broadband service that offers fast internet at low cost. Their app should let users do everything a broadband customer needs: check usage, renew plans, pay bills, and get support, without ever picking up a phone. It didn't.

The existing app had unclear navigation, unlabelled icons, a punishingly long renewal flow, and a customer support path so confusing that users called instead of using it. I redesigned the experience end to end, alongside the marketing website.

The Challenge

The issues weren't subtle

  1. 0 1

    Navigation was unclear and inconsistent across surfaces.

  2. 0 2

    Icons were unlabelled, forcing users to guess at meaning.

  3. 0 3

    The renewal process took too many steps for the most-used action.

  4. 0 4

    Basic tasks were always difficult, with friction at every turn.

  5. 0 5

    Reaching out to customer support was confusing and unguided.

  6. 0 6

    Information architecture was fragmented and didn't reflect customer mental models.

What I Fixed

The redesign in one list

  1. 0 1

    Fixed a bottom navigation tab with clear icons and labels.

  2. 0 2

    Introduced new icons with text labels to eliminate guesswork.

  3. 0 3

    Simplified renewal into the minimum number of steps.

  4. 0 4

    Redesigned basic tasks to feel simpler, and a little more fun.

  5. 0 5

    Made reaching customer service clearer and more human.

  6. 0 6

    Restructured information architecture so the app feels predictable.

My Approach

How I worked through it

  1. 0 1

    Anchor the experience with clear navigation

    Implemented a fixed bottom navigation with labels so the app felt predictable, with confident movement between billing, support, and account.

  2. 0 2

    Turn renewal into a visible goal

    Moved plan renewal onto the home screen as a dedicated flow, making the task obvious and reducing the steps needed to complete it.

  3. 0 3

    Make support feel human and accessible

    Redesigned customer care wording and flow so users felt supported rather than lost, turning support into a usable product feature, not a dead end to a phone call.

  4. 0 4

    One product, every surface

    Redesigned the marketing website alongside the mobile app to share visual language, copy voice, and information architecture.

What I Learned

Three takeaways

  1. 0 1

    Listing the problems is half the work

    I started by writing every single thing wrong with the existing app. The act of enumeration surfaces patterns you can't see when issues stay in your head. I now do this for every product I inherit.

  2. 0 2

    The most-used flow deserves the most attention

    Subscriptions and renewals are the boring, repeated, every-month task. They are also the highest-stakes UX moments in a broadband product. The hierarchy of attention should match the hierarchy of use.

  3. 0 3

    Tone is a feature

    'Talk to one of our customer care agents' isn't decoration. It's the difference between a user trying the path and a user calling support. Microcopy is a product decision.
